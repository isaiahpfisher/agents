COURSE DESCRIPTION
==================================================
An introduction to AI and its applications in business and society. The course has three major objectives:
1. Explore key AI, ML, and generative AI concepts.
2. Apply AI tools for real-world problem-solving.
3. Evaluate the ethical and societal impacts of AI technologies.
Topics include retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), generative models, and multimodal AI, with a focus on business value and industry use cases.

Prerequisites: There are no required or recommended prerequisites.

Major requirements this course fulfills:
- Required upper-division course for Computer Science with AI majors.
- Computer Science upper-division elective for other emphases.

==================================================
COURSE OUTCOMES
==================================================
By the end of the course, students will be able to:
1. Understand key AI, ML, and generative AI concepts, including retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), generative models, and multimodal AI.
   Assessed by: Homework. Program outcome: CS Knowledge.
2. Apply AI tools to generate materials for real-world applications such as content creation, research, and data synthesis that provide business value.
   Assessed by: Homework. Program outcomes: CS Knowledge, CS Skills, CS Career & Vocation.
3. Evaluate ethical considerations, biases, and the broader societal effects of AI technologies in business.
   Assessed by: Quizzes, Discussions, Homework. Program outcomes: CS Knowledge, CS Christian Worldview, CS Career & Vocation.
4. Evaluate the latest AI advancements and their potential implications for various industries.
   Assessed by: Quizzes, Discussions, Homework. Program outcomes: CS Christian Worldview, CS Career & Vocation.
5. Identify potential use cases for AI in specific industries.
   Assessed by: Discussions, Homework. Program outcomes: CS Knowledge, CS Career & Vocation.

COURSE POLICIES
==================================================
Generative AI policy:
The course encourages students to explore generative AI. Most assignments will require it, because learning to use automated tools successfully and ethically is an emerging skill.

Attendance, tardiness, and leaving early:
No official tally of absences or tardiness directly affects the grade. However, attendance affects classroom participation grades and quiz grades.

Late assignment policy:
All assignments are turned in online, so there should be little excuse for late work due to technical or logistical reasons. Late deductions:
- Up to 1 day (24 hours) late: -10%
- 1 day to less than 7 days late: -20%
- 7 days to less than 14 days late: -30%
- 14 days to less than 30 days late: -40%
- 30 or more days late: -100%
The instructor may grant exceptions or reductions on a case-by-case basis.
Assignments are not accepted after the last day of the course (December 10).

Inclement weather policy:
If a class period or exam is missed because campus is closed for bad weather, the professor will determine a reasonable makeup plan based on what was missed.

Campus closing policy:
Because most students and professors live on or very near campus, the University does not suspend classes for bad weather unless the conditions pose unnecessary risk to faculty, staff, and students. Students prevented from attending class because of road conditions should notify their professor of the cause, and the absence will be treated as being for an acceptable reason.

Emergency statement:
In an emergency, faculty, staff, and students signed up for OC Campus Messenger (oc.edu/notify) will be notified by text, email, or Twitter. If an emergency occurs during class, the instructor will tell students present the appropriate procedures.

Additional OC policies:
